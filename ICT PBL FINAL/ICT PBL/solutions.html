Extreme heat can affect human health, crop growth, livestock and household income. Preparing in advance helps communities reduce risks, use resources wisely and respond more effectively when conditions become dangerous.

Get Ready Before Temperatures Rise

Simple preparations can help protect people, crops and essential resources during periods of extreme heat.

1| PERSONAL SAFETY

Prepare for the Heat

Keep safe drinking water available, identify cool or shaded places, wear lightweight clothing and plan outdoor work for cooler hours.

2 | FARM PREPARATION

Practical Solutions for Farmers

Detailed breakdown of climate-resilient agricultural strategies & suitable crop selections for Central Myanmar.

1

Use Water Efficiently

High temperatures and erratic rainfall make strategic water preservation essential to maintain crop yields and prevent severe thermal stress.

Soil Moisture Monitoring

Check dampness at 15cm root depth before watering. Using simple tensiometers or soil depth feelers avoids over-irrigation and root degradation.

Scheduled Irrigation

Irrigate strictly during early morning (5 AM – 8 AM) or late evening. This ensures root absorption before peak sunlight triggers rapid evaporation.

Drip & Precision Delivery

Direct water straight to crop roots via low-cost drip tubing. This cuts evaporation waste by 40–60% compared to traditional flood irrigation.

Farm Ponds & Water Storage

Construct lined earth ponds (*Jalkunds*) to harvest excess monsoon runoff for emergency supplemental watering during prolonged dry spells.

Alternate Wetting & Drying (AWD)

For flooded fields, allow soil to dry periodically before re-flooding to conserve water resources without compromising vegetative growth.

Sub-surface Drip Tubing

Install buried drip lines directly within root zones, completely shielding irrigation water from surface heat and wind-driven evaporation.

2

Protect the Soil

Maintaining healthy, shaded soil stops moisture loss, regulates micro-climate temperatures, and shields beneficial soil microbes from scorching heatwaves.

Organic Mulching

Apply a 5-10cm layer of paddy straw, groundnut shells, or dry leaves over topsoil to drop ground temperature by up to 5°C and conserve moisture.

Leguminous Cover Crops

Plant fast-growing legumes like Cowpea between main rows to shade topsoil, fix natural nitrogen, and reduce wind-driven soil erosion.

Zero / Minimum Tillage

Avoid deep plowing during heatwaves to maintain soil crust integrity, lock in subsurface moisture, and preserve delicate soil fungal networks.

Biochar Soil Enrichment

Incorporate localized crop-waste biochar into topsoil to boost water retention capacity, improve aeration, and nourish beneficial soil microbes.

Contour Bunding & Terracing

Build earthen ridges along sloping farmland to slow down heavy rainwater run-off, encouraging deep soil infiltration and preventing topsoil loss.

Agroforestry & Windbreaks

Plant rows of multi-purpose drought-resistant trees around field boundaries to block harsh drying winds and create localized cooling shade.

3

Choose Suitable Crops

Selecting resilient plant varieties and shifting agricultural practices mitigates total crop failure risks during extreme heatwaves and dry spells.

Heat & Drought Tolerance

Transition to deep-rooted, low-water-demand crops that maintain stomatal control and photosynthesis under high ambient temperatures.

Adjusted Planting Windows

Shift sowing schedules forward or backward based on localized meteorological forecasts rather than strictly following traditional calendars.

Crop Diversification & Intercropping

Intercrop multiple varieties (e.g., Sesame with Pigeon Pea) to safeguard farm income if one crop suffers thermal stress.

Recommended Crops for Heatwaves & Dry Zones

Sorghum (ပြောင်းဆန်)
Cereal

Extremely heat-tolerant with deep root systems. Uses 30% less water than maize and withstands soil temperatures up to 38°C.

Pigeon Pea (ပဲစင်းငုံ)
Legume

Deep taproots penetrate subsoil layers to access hidden moisture reserves, surviving severe heatwaves where standard pulses fail.

Pearl Millet (မြက်ပြောင်း)
Millet

Thrives in low-nutrient sandy soils and ambient heat reaching 42°C with a short 60–75 day maturity cycle.

Sesame (နှမ်း)
Oilseed

Drought-resistant short-duration oilseed. Indigenous varieties show high thermal tolerance during early growth stages.

Green Gram(ပဲတီစိမ်း)
Pulse

Fast 60-day maturation period avoids long heatwaves, providing a reliable catch-crop while enriching nitrogen levels.

Cassava & Sweet Potato
Tuber

Underground tuber development protects edible yield from scorching surface heat waves, providing a resilient fallback crop.

Know How to Respond

Recognising dangerous conditions and acting early can help prevent serious harm.

1 FOR PEOPLE

Protect Human Health

  • Move to a cool or shaded place.
  • Drink water regularly.
  • Pause strenuous activity during peak heat.
  • Check on vulnerable people.
  • Seek urgent medical help for suspected heatstroke.
2 FOR FARMERS

Reduce Agricultural Losses

  • Check crops for heat stress and wilting.
  • Prioritise available water carefully.
  • Follow local agricultural advice.
  • Protect livestock with shade and safe drinking water.
  • Record crop damage and changing conditions.
3 FOR COMMUNITIES

Support One Another

  • Share verified weather warnings.
  • Help people who may need assistance.
  • Identify accessible cool places.
  • Report urgent health concerns.
  • Follow guidance from local authorities.

PRACTICAL ACTIONS

Explore Heatwave Solutions

Discover practical ways to protect people, farms, and communities before, during, and after extreme heat.

Preparing early can reduce the risks of extreme heat and help families and farmers protect their resources.

  • Check weather forecasts and heat warnings regularly.
  • Store safe drinking water and prepare a cool, shaded place.
  • Plan outdoor work for cooler hours, such as early morning.
  • Prepare emergency contacts and check on vulnerable neighbours.
  • Farmers can plan water use and prepare shade for livestock.

  • Drink water regularly and rest in a cool or shaded area.
  • Wear lightweight, breathable clothing.
  • Reduce strenuous activity during the hottest hours.
  • Never leave children or pets inside a parked vehicle.
  • Give livestock access to clean water and shade.
  • Watch for dizziness, headaches, nausea, and unusual weakness.
Recognise an emergency: Confusion, seizures, or loss of consciousness during extreme heat may indicate heatstroke. Seek emergency medical help immediately and begin cooling the person while help is coming.

Extreme heat can increase water demand, stress crops and livestock, and reduce agricultural productivity.

  • Water crops according to their needs and local conditions.
  • Use mulch where suitable to help soil retain moisture.
  • Maintain shade and ventilation for livestock.
  • Provide animals with reliable access to clean drinking water.
  • Monitor crops for heat stress and adjust farm activities when possible.
  • Discuss locally suitable crop varieties and planting schedules with agricultural advisers.

  • Share verified heat warnings with family, friends, and neighbours.
  • Check on older adults, young children, and people who work outdoors.
  • Encourage schools and workplaces to provide water, shade, and rest breaks.
  • Plant and protect trees where appropriate to increase shade over time.
  • Share reliable information instead of spreading unverified advice.

Small actions matter: Communities can reduce heat-related risks when people share information and look out for one another.

  • Continue drinking water and allow time for recovery.
  • Seek medical advice if symptoms persist or worsen.
  • Check crops and livestock for signs of heat-related damage.
  • Review water supplies and identify what could be improved.
  • Record lessons learned to improve preparation for future heatwaves.
